A visual system connecting identity, accessible production, and printed and digital experiences.

Context

An identity that grew across new touchpoints

The project began with the development of a graphic identifier and wedding invitations under an important premise: finding accessible production solutions without sacrificing the visual character of the pieces. As the identity began to take shape, the project expanded into new touchpoints: a web invitation and, finally, a custom digital experience for the gift registry.

Condensed brief

Four decisions that shaped the system

Build a distinctive identity

Develop an identifier that preserved the elegance associated with a wedding while connecting with the couple's cultural references and personal history.

Design with production in mind

Keep printing costs under control without sacrificing visual detail by considering material selection, illustration complexity, and cutting feasibility from the design stage.

Unify different media

Extend the identity created for the physical pieces into a website based on an existing template, retaining shared elements without requiring both media to look identical.

Solve a specific gifting experience

Create an alternative that allowed guests to fund experiences through monetary contributions without relying on a commercial platform, while keeping the flow straightforward.

Identity

From sketches to a distinctive visual language

As the project's visual language took shape, the identifier became a more personal solution: a seal inspired by Japanese graphic tradition and by the convergence of the couple's Japanese and Latin American roots. The result works as a distinctive element that extends beyond the invitation and brings cohesion to the identity across printed and digital pieces.

Printed invitations

From paper tests to a physical piece

The invitation was developed through a series of preliminary sketches and an initial physical dummy. From these tests, the illustrations were refined and vectorized with cutting feasibility in mind, preserving the graphic detail and elegant character of the proposal while accounting for production and budget constraints.

Digital experience

From paper to screen

The digital experience began with a Canva template previously selected by the couple. Instead of literally reproducing the printed composition, the template was adapted to the visual system developed for the project, using the color palette, seal, and illustrations as elements of continuity.

[Main website asset 20 pending]

A tailored experience

The gift registry presented a different need: the couple wanted to offer experiences instead of physical gifts and let guests make full or partial monetary contributions without relying on a commercial platform. To support this flow, a custom web application was developed, visually integrated with the rest of the system and connected to a database that manages contributions and the availability of each experience.
